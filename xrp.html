2 · How a swap works, with one real case

THORChain is a protocol to change one coin into another across networks. To change XRP into Bitcoin, you send XRP to one of its vaults with a note (a memo) that says where to pay. THORChain then pays Bitcoin to that address and writes the hash of your XRP payment in the Bitcoin transaction. That is why we can match both sides exactly, not by amount or time.

THORChain has 6 XRP vaults: rJpKWz…, rMTwKk…, rPNjkN…, rf4ixB…, rsUhJL…, rwA6nv…. We checked them against the THORChain public API. They were created on 11 Oct, before the hack, as part of the normal change of vaults; they belong to the protocol, not to the attacker.

When the price is not good enough, THORChain does not swap and sends the XRP back with the memo REFUND:<hash>. Most memos ask to split the swap in up to 255 small swaps (…/0/255): THORChain does the small swaps that meet the price and returns the rest. Two examples from rnkJEb…: on 5 Nov 20:46 it sent 100,000 XRP and got 9,142.65 back, so only part was changed (see refund); on 9 Nov 12:37 it sent 74,000 XRP and got 73,999.77 back, all of it minus a fee of 0.23 XRP (see refund).

3 · From 1 wallet to 78 Bitcoin addresses

ryB7NE… sent 233.6M XRP to 98 wallets. 57 of them sent XRP to THORChain: 6,378 swap tries, 315.4M XRP. THORChain sent 114.4M back in 3,937 refunds, so the net is 200.9M XRP: 200.0M to Bitcoin and 0.91M to Ethereum.

Patterns

  1. One Bitcoin address for each wallet. 71 of the 78 Bitcoin addresses get paid by only one wallet, and 42 of the 56 wallets pay only one address. Each wallet works like a separate box.
  2. More than half of the tries get money back, and they try again. 3,937 of 6,378 tries (62%) got a REFUND, because the memo asked for a minimum price that the pool could not always give: 3,552 were changed only in part and 385 not at all. The wallet then sends the returned XRP again. That is the "back and forth" you see in the explorer.
  3. Round amounts out, broken amounts back. 87% of the XRP sent to THORChain are whole numbers, chosen by a person or a script. The refunds are never whole numbers.
  4. Few side exits. Outside THORChain there are only small amounts: 26,027 XRP to the Flare Core Vault and 14,000 XRP through the Axelar bridge to the XRPL EVM network. A few payments carry a destination tag (a deposit at a service), for small amounts.
  5. Bots that send dust. Many 0.00001 XRP payments arrive from addresses that start like the real ones. This is "address poisoning": bots hope someone copies the wrong address. They are not part of the theft, and our filter of 1,000 XRP leaves them out.

6 · After Bitcoin: spread, parked and mixed

We took all 78 Bitcoin addresses that THORChain paid. THORChain paid them 3,424 BTC, and only that counts as the trail: money they got from other senders is left out. We followed them forward, up to 3 more steps, in payments of 0.5 BTC or more. Data up to 20 Nov 10:37 UTC.

  1. They spread it again. In the first step the money goes to 1,187 new addresses with 0.5 BTC or more. Most payments are simple: one output and the change. None goes back to THORChain. We did not follow all of them. We followed the 1,000 that got the most (3,178 BTC, 95% of it). The other 187 got 170 BTC, 1.42 BTC or less each, and we did not follow them.
  2. Then the money waits. 880 of the 1,376 addresses we followed got money and never spent it. Together they hold 2,663.0 BTC, often in round amounts (87 of them hold a whole number of BTC). New addresses with one payment and round amounts look like money put aside, out of sight of the 70 addresses that THORChain made public.
  3. Some of it is mixed. bc1qlkyxv6…fy0v put about 39.8 BTC into one transaction with 237 inputs and 368 outputs, many of them with the same amount (for example 0.00016384 BTC, that is 214 satoshis). This is the shape of a CoinJoin: many people mix their coins in one transaction, so no one can say which output belongs to whom. 55 more addresses further down the trail do the same, with about 155.6 BTC. After a CoinJoin, the trail cannot be followed with certainty. Still, it is a small part: most of the money is parked, not mixed.
  4. Some roads meet again. 190 addresses get money from two or more of the starting addresses. The biggest is bc1qwx0xdq…zjnc: 72.9 BTC from 3 of them, and then it spreads it again.
  5. Several addresses move in the same block. In 189 blocks, three or more of the starting addresses spend at the same time. This is not proof of one owner, but it fits one person or one script.

9 · Data and method
