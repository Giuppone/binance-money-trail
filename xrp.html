Where the stolen XRP goes

Most of it is already in Bitcoin. It went through THORChain, one Bitcoin address per wallet, and part of it ends in the same address as the stolen SOL.

Author: Cristian Giuppone

What this page is and is not. It follows the XRP with public data only: the XRP Ledger, THORChain and Bitcoin. Every step has a link, so anyone can check it.

It does not identify people. The wallets are only addresses. THORChain is a public protocol used by many people; its vaults are not the attacker's wallets. This is a research and teaching exercise, not a forensic report for a court.

1 · The dates

On 29 Oct, five Binance wallets sent 233,461,943 XRP to rK21t6…. On 2 Nov, in 13 minutes, it split the XRP into 6 wallets. Then nothing moved for three days.

On 5 Nov 14:56 UTC, one of the six, ryB7NE…, started to send XRP to new wallets. The first swap into Bitcoin came a few minutes later. Three other wallets of the split refilled ryB7NE…: rKxwzj… sent 40M XRP on 6 Nov 06:06 (See tx); rhfA8Q… sent 40M XRP on 7 Nov 02:50 (See tx); rsNoNk… sent 40M XRP on 8 Nov 23:53 (See tx); rhXtRt… sent 40M XRP on 9 Nov 17:18 (See tx); raoMrx… sent 33M XRP on 11 Nov 00:01 (See tx).

The swaps follow the spreading by minutes, day and night. The longest stop was from 7 Nov 15:50 to 8 Nov 14:46 UTC.

2 · How a swap works, with one real case

THORChain is a protocol to change one coin into another across networks. To change XRP into Bitcoin, you send XRP to one of its vaults with a note (a memo) that says where to pay. THORChain then pays Bitcoin to that address and writes the hash of your XRP payment in the Bitcoin transaction. That is why we can match both sides exactly, not by amount or time.

THORChain has 6 XRP vaults: rJpKWz…, rMTwKk…, rPNjkN…, rf4ixB…, rsUhJL…, rwA6nv…. We checked them against the THORChain public API. They were created on 11 Oct, before the hack, as part of the normal change of vaults; they belong to the protocol, not to the attacker.

    "=" means swap, "b" means Bitcoin, then the Bitcoin address that gets paid, then the minimum it accepts (4.6139 BTC, written in satoshis) and how to split the swap (1/0). See tx
  5. THORChain
  6. 6 Nov 06:44Pays 4.6523 BTC to bc1qa5n7qr…2dxr. The Bitcoin transaction carries the note OUT:4F46AD720F…: the hash of the XRP deposit above. See tx

When the price is not good enough, THORChain does not swap and sends the XRP back with the memo REFUND:<hash>. Most memos ask to split the swap in up to 255 small swaps (…/0/255): THORChain does the small swaps that meet the price and returns the rest. Two examples from rnkJEb…: on 5 Nov 20:46 it sent 100,000 XRP and got 9,142.65 back, so only part was changed (see refund); on 9 Nov 12:37 it sent 74,000 XRP and got 73,999.77 back, all of it minus a fee of 0.23 XRP (see refund).

3 · From 1 wallet to 78 Bitcoin addresses

ryB7NE… sent 226.7M XRP to 96 wallets. 56 of them sent XRP to THORChain: 6,040 swap tries, 302.7M XRP. THORChain sent 110.9M back in 3,661 refunds, so the net is 191.9M XRP: 191.2M to Bitcoin and 0.69M to Ethereum.

Patterns

  1. One Bitcoin address for each wallet. 71 of the 78 Bitcoin addresses get paid by only one wallet, and 42 of the 56 wallets pay only one address. Each wallet works like a separate box.
  2. More than half of the tries get money back, and they try again. 3,661 of 6,040 tries (61%) got a REFUND, because the memo asked for a minimum price that the pool could not always give: 3,283 were changed only in part and 378 not at all. The wallet then sends the returned XRP again. That is the "back and forth" you see in the explorer.
  3. Round amounts out, broken amounts back. 87% of the XRP sent to THORChain are whole numbers, chosen by a person or a script. The refunds are never whole numbers.
  4. Few side exits. Outside THORChain there are only small amounts: 26,027 XRP to the Flare Core Vault and 14,000 XRP through the Axelar bridge to the XRPL EVM network. A few payments carry a destination tag (a deposit at a service), for small amounts.
  5. Bots that send dust. Many 0.00001 XRP payments arrive from addresses that start like the real ones. This is "address poisoning": bots hope someone copies the wrong address. They are not part of the theft, and our filter of 1,000 XRP leaves them out.

6 · After Bitcoin: spread, parked and mixed

We took the 8 Bitcoin addresses that got the most (2,067 of 3,425 BTC) and followed them forward, up to 3 more steps, in payments of 0.5 BTC or more. Data up to 10 Nov 00:22 UTC.

  1. They spread it again. In the first step the money goes to 455 new addresses with 0.5 BTC or more. Most payments are simple: one output and the change. None goes back to THORChain.
  2. Then the money waits. 456 of the 492 addresses we followed got money and never spent it. Together they hold 1,599.1 BTC, often in round amounts (88 of them hold a whole number of BTC). New addresses with one payment and round amounts look like money put aside, out of sight of the 70 addresses that THORChain made public.
  3. Some of it is mixed. bc1q5hjy0s…8ec6 put about 25.6 BTC into 4 transactions with 134 to 284 inputs and 175 to 294 outputs, many of them with the same amount (for example 0.00016384 BTC, that is 214 satoshis). This is the shape of a CoinJoin: many people mix their coins in one transaction, so no one can say which output belongs to whom. 8 more addresses further down the trail do the same, with about 9.0 BTC. After a CoinJoin, the trail cannot be followed with certainty. Still, it is a small part: most of the money is parked, not mixed.
  4. Some roads meet again. 6 addresses get money from two or more of the starting addresses. The biggest is bc1qwx0xdq…zjnc: 73.1 BTC from 3 of them, and then it spreads it again.
  5. Several addresses move in the same block. In 55 blocks, three or more of the starting addresses spend at the same time. This is not proof of one owner, but it fits one person or one script.

8 · What we cannot say

  • Who controls these wallets. We only see addresses and how money moves between them.
  • That every Bitcoin address belongs to the attacker. They get paid with stolen XRP, but an address can also be a service.
  • Where all the Bitcoin goes next. We followed only the addresses that got the most, and only payments of 0.5 BTC or more. After a CoinJoin, the trail is not certain.
  • Payments under 1,000 XRP. We leave them out to avoid dust; small amounts may be missing.

9 · Data and method

XRP Ledger: public nodes (account_tx) and XRPSCAN for names and balances. THORChain: memos in the XRP payments and the public API (/thorchain/vaults/asgard). Bitcoin: mempool.space. We follow only native XRP, in payments of 1,000 XRP or more. A refund is matched to its swap by the hash in the memo. The Bitcoin side is matched by the hash in each THORChain payment (OP_RETURN "OUT:<hash>"). After that, in Bitcoin, we follow the outputs of each transaction that spends an address of the trail, in proportion to what that address put in.
